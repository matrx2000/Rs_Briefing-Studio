Meta Set the audience first — every other field is written for them. The decision-by date should be a real deadline; vague deadlines invite vague replies.

Bottom line up front The reader has 90 seconds. The headline must state the decision you want, with the headline number embedded. Supporting sentence (≤1150 chars) answers "why this, why now". If you can't fit it, the framing isn't sharp yet.

Headline metric ONE number that captures the "so what". Pick the metric that, if wrong, kills the proposal. Keep value short (it auto-scales but readability suffers past 6 chars).

Image position & fit are PDF-layout settings — not applicable in Markdown mode.

Commentary Optional 1–2 short paragraphs framing the decision. Use sparingly — page 1 is for skimming, not narrative. If the reader needs more than this, push detail to page 2.

5 Ws + H Facts, not arguments. One short clause per cell. If a cell needs a full sentence, you're explaining — move that to page 2.

Pros (2–4) Rank by importance and stop at 4. Write each as an outcome the reader can verify ("Saves €40k/yr"), not a feature ("modern infrastructure").

Cons (2–4) Name the real risks, not strawmen. The reader trusts your pros only as much as they trust your cons. Mitigations belong on page 2.

Timeline (4 phases) 4 phases, one line each. Use real months or sprint names. The progress bar shows where you are right now — not where you plan to be.

The Ask / CTA ONE decision, ONE deadline. If you can't state the ask in a short sentence, you don't know what you're asking for. The button text is the deadline reminder.

Page 2 — Context Read time ~3–5 min. Depth on demand: only readers who need the full story open this. Keep prose tight; use bullets for considerations and risks, prose for background.
